EE Mindset · Today

From supervisor to commander

As Dy.EE you supervised work; as EE you own outcomes. The shift is not technical — it is psychological. Three habits separate a strong EE from a struggling one:
  1. Review by exception. Don't read every file — read the outliers. Ask AEs to flag deviations, not to dump data.
  2. Lead the calendar, don't follow it. Block first hours for inspections & reviews. If you reach office only to attend visitors, the division will run you.
  3. Decide on paper. Verbal orders die quietly. Every approval, every refusal — initial it, date it, with reasoning.

The 30-day billing cycle — what really happens

From the consumer's meter to your collection register, the money passes through 11 hands and 4 systems. Each handoff is a potential leakage point. As EE, your job is not to do billing — it is to verify that no step is silently failing.

1
Meter reading (MRI / spot billing / AMR)
Reader uploads CMRI dump or spot-bills via handheld. Risk: "stuck-meter" cases marked as average — 90% of theft hides here.
2
Reading validation in SAP IS-U
System auto-flags abnormals (deviation > 50% vs avg). Risk: AE bypasses with "Accepted" — verify rejection log monthly.
3
Bill generation & DPC
Tariff applied, demand charges added (HT/LT industrial), bill printed/SMS pushed. Risk: wrong category = ARR loss, audit para.
4
Collection (counters, online, agencies)
ERO, eSeva, app, BBPS, agents. Risk: agency lag — money received but not yet posted (timing gap = DCB mismatch).
5
Reconciliation (DCB closure)
D = Demand, C = Collection, B = Balance. Monthly DCB must reconcile to last paisa with bank scroll, eSeva, agency report.
6
Disconnection notice → Action
After 15 days arrear → notice; 30 days → disconnection. EE failure: notices issued but disconnection never executed = soft division, arrears compound.
💀 The 3 silent killers
  • 1. Reader marks "premises locked" month after month → consumer using power free
  • 2. Stuck-meter average ≠ actual consumption (motors, ACs added)
  • 3. Category remains LT-1 (domestic) when premises now commercial
✓ Strong EE practice
  • Pull 20 random "locked-premises" records every month — physically verify 5
  • Review stuck-meter list before bill cycle close — replace within 30 days
  • Cross-check GST/Shops Act database for hidden commercial premises
Why this matters

One missed cycle = compounding loss. A defective meter unbilled for 6 months on a 5 HP motor leakage = ~₹18,000 unrecovered. Now multiply by 200 such cases. That's ₹36 lakhs/year walking out of your division silently.

Hierarchy

Meter classes & where they sit

1
Boundary / Feeder meter — at 33/11 kV substation outgoing feeder. Establishes energy input to a feeder. Critical for feeder-wise loss calculation.
2
DTR meter — at distribution transformer LT side. Mandatory under PFC/REC norms. Difference between DTR input and sum of consumer meters = DTR loss + theft.
3
HT consumer meter — TVM (Trivector). Records kWh, kVAh, kVArh, MD. CT/PT operated. Accuracy class 0.5s.
4
LT-CT meter — for loads > 5 kW (commercial) or as per regulation. CT operated, class 1.
5
LT whole-current meter — domestic, small commercial. Class 1 or 2 depending on category.
Practical reality

CT/PT ratio — the #1 cause of revenue loss in HT

When an HT consumer's CT ratio is set wrong in the meter or in SAP, billing is silently wrong — often by 10× or 100×. This is the single most expensive mistake in DISCOM history.

Worked example

M/s ABC Industries: HT meter has 200/5A CT (ratio 40), but SAP master shows 100/5A (ratio 20). Every kWh recorded is billed at ½ actual. Monthly consumption 80,000 kWh × ₹8 = ₹6.4 L billed. Actual should be ₹12.8 L. Loss: ₹6.4 L per month, often undetected for years.

EE control measure

Quarterly HT meter audit: physically read CT nameplate, compare with meter MF, compare with SAP MF. All three must match. Sign off with date. Keep register.

Defective vs Stuck — know the difference
Stuck meter

Reading not advancing. Bill on average of last 3 cycles. Replace within 30 days. Investigate if recurrence in same premises.

Defective meter

Runs but error > permissible. Identified during meter testing. Bill on actual error % correction for last 6 months (regulated period).

Burnt / Tampered

Tampered → Section 135 (theft) — vigilance case. Burnt → ascertain cause; if external (lightning, overload) replace and recover normally.

CMD breach (Contracted Maximum Demand)

If MD > CMD → 2× demand charges on excess (regulated). Habitual breach → revise agreement upward, no choice for consumer. Don't let it slide — that's leakage AND grid safety risk.

Penalty per regulator. Counsel consumer to install capacitor bank. Better for grid (less I²R loss) AND legit revenue from penalty until they fix.

Where money silently disappears

Revenue Leakage — the 12 hidden drains

1. Direct theft (hooking)

Tapping from LT line. Visible in nightly inspection. Pareto: 5-12% of total energy in vulnerable areas.

2. Meter tampering

Magnet, slow-down devices, reverse polarity, neutral bypass. Detect via load survey vs billing.

3. CT/PT ratio mismatch

Wrong MF in SAP — silent killer for HT. Detect via quarterly audit.

4. Category misclassification

Commercial premises billed as domestic. GST/Shops Act cross-check.

5. Defective meters unreplaced

"Average billing" indefinitely. Set 30-day SLA for replacement.

6. Locked-premises chronic

Reader marks locked 6+ cycles. Physically verify — usually power being used.

7. Unbilled connections

Service released but never entered in SAP. SC-wise reconcile with field register.

8. DTR loss masked as theft

Aged DTR → high I²R. Verify oil/coil health before chasing theft.

9. Wrong tariff applied

Tariff revision not loaded; old rates billed. Check SAP rate table on tariff order date.

10. CMD under-recorded

Meter MD reset issue or integration period wrong → demand charges under-billed.

11. Adjustment abuse

Junior officer giving "adjustments" without basis — vigilance risk. Approval matrix mandatory.

12. Collection lag / agency fraud

Cash collected by agent, not remitted. Daily remittance verification, surprise checks.

Section 135 — IE Act

Theft handling — protocol that survives court

A weakly documented theft case collapses in court and emboldens the entire area. Stronger documentation = stronger deterrence than the fine itself.

1
Inspection team — AE + one independent witness (panch). Police presence advised in tense areas.
2
Spot Mahazar drawn — describe tampering precisely, take photographs from multiple angles, video if possible.
3
Meter sealed and removed with seal numbers recorded; consumer signature taken (or refused — noted).
4
Assessment — per regulation (typically 12 months × calculated load × 2 for theft). Provisional → final after hearing.
5
FIR / Special Court — file within statutory limit. Meter testing at MRT lab — joint inspection.
6
Hearing & order — consumer's reply considered, speaking order issued by Assessing Officer.
7
Appeal — to Appellate Authority within 30 days. If consumer doesn't appeal & doesn't pay → recovery as arrears of land revenue.
EE's role in theft cases
  • • Don't lead the raid — let vigilance/AE do field work. Your job: review, approve, ratify.
  • • Sign assessment orders only after seeing photographs, mahazar, meter test report.
  • • Beware political pressure — document any verbal request to "settle"; never reduce assessment without written ground.
  • • Track conviction rate — if < 30%, mahazars are weak; train AEs on documentation.
Most common mistakes
  1. Witness signature missing → entire case voided
  2. Meter sent to lab without joint seal → tampering of evidence alleged
  3. Assessment based on "previous bill" rather than connected load → reduced in appeal
  4. FIR delayed beyond regulation timeline → procedural challenge succeeds
  5. No hearing notice served → natural justice violated
Quasi-judicial role

Assessment officer authority — wield it carefully

Under Sections 126 (unauthorized use) and 135 (theft), the designated officer (often EE) acts in a quasi-judicial capacity. Your order must be a speaking order — reasoned, factual, citing law — or it collapses in appeal.

Section 126 — Unauthorized use

Connection exists but used differently (e.g., domestic premises running commercial activity). Assessment = 1.5× tariff difference for last 12 months. No criminal case — civil.

Section 135 — Theft

Tampering, hooking, bypass. Assessment + criminal case. Compoundable on payment of full assessed amount + compounding fee.

Section 138 — Meter interference

Damaging/destroying meter. Often invoked alongside 135.

Section 56 — Disconnection notice

15-day notice mandatory for arrear-based disconnection. Skip = consumer wins court case.

Structure of a defensible speaking order
  1. Facts — date of inspection, team, what was found (verbatim from mahazar)
  2. Evidence — photographs, meter test report, load survey, witness statement
  3. Consumer's reply — quoted, each contention addressed
  4. Legal provisions invoked — section, regulation clause
  5. Calculation of assessment — formula, multiplier, period, load basis
  6. Finding & order — sum payable, mode, time limit, appeal route
Tariff = the regulator's signature

Tariff implementation — what changes on order day

When the SERC issues a tariff order (usually annual), thousands of rate combinations change. Mistakes cascade across lakhs of bills. The EE's job is to verify implementation, not to do it.

1
Tariff order received from CO — note effective date
2
SAP rate table updated by IT/CO — verify with sample bills before bulk run
3
Subsidy mapping reset (agriculture, weaver, etc.) — confirm with state govt circular
4
Connection charges, FRC, security deposit slabs revised — update at counters
5
Run trial bills for each category — compare with calculator
6

Category conversion (LT-1 → LT-2/LT-3)

When premises use changes (domestic → commercial). Requires: spot inspection, photograph, recommendation note, sanction by competent authority, retrospective recovery if abuse detected.

Wrong category red flags

High consumption on domestic (> 500 units in commercial-prone area), commercial premises with LT-1, GST registration vs LT-1, industrial address as residential.

Career-damaging errors

Revenue mistakes that have ended EE careers

▶ Signing DCB without sample reconciliation

Junior staff cooks up DCB to show clean figures. EE signs blindly. Two years later, audit finds ₹3 Cr ghost collections. Vigilance case. Always pick 5-10 random entries each month and trace bill→bank→SAP.

▶ Reducing assessment without written grounds

Phone call from politician. EE reduces ₹15 L assessment to ₹2 L. No written ground for reduction. Vigilance discovers — bribery presumption. Reduce only on appeal/written representation citing specific factual error.

▶ Ignoring chronic locked-premises

Reader marks premises locked every cycle for 8 months. EE never asks. CAG audit finds active loads on these premises. Para opens. Auto-flag > 3 cycles locked, mandate physical verification.

▶ Verbal disconnection / reconnection orders

"Telephone instruction" to disconnect HT consumer over dispute. Consumer sues for revenue loss. EE personally liable. Every disconnection in writing, with notice trail.

▶ Accepting "settled" by previous officer

New EE inherits long-pending arrears. Predecessor said "old, written-off, ignore." But there is no formal write-off order. Audit picks it up — current EE held accountable for non-action. Demand the write-off order or treat as live arrear.

Your accountability boundary

As EE (Operations), you typically own the 33/11 kV SS, all 11 kV feeders, all DTRs, all LT lines, all consumer service mains. The 220/132 kV upstream is TRANSCO (or another wing). Know the demarcation point exactly — it matters during outage attribution.

Critical "knots" — single points of failure

Each 11 kV outgoing breaker at the SS feeds 1,500-8,000 consumers. Each DTR feeds 50-200. The asymmetry matters: a tripped feeder ≠ a tripped DTR. Communications, resourcing, and political sensitivity scale very differently.

11 kV feeders = your reputation

Feeder management — beyond just "tripping"

1
Feeder profiling — every feeder must have a documented profile: route map, length, conductor type, number of DTRs, connected load, critical consumers, history of trips.
2
Loading status — verified twice yearly. Over-loaded (> 80% capacity) feeders need bifurcation or augmentation. Don't let a feeder go to 100% — that's a breakdown waiting.
3
Trip history analysis — sort feeders by trips/month. Top 5 chronic trippers get a root-cause review every quarter.
4
Patrolling cycle — pre-monsoon walk-through, mid-summer thermal scan, post-monsoon damage survey.
5
Reliability mapping — every feeder has SAIDI / SAIFI / CAIDI. Track over time. Public-facing index in many states.

DTR lifecycle

From sanction to disposal — track every transformer

A DTR is the most replicated asset in your division (typically 800-2,500 units). Its lifecycle has 10 stages — and audit objections cluster around stages 2, 6, 9.

1. Sanction
Load demand assessment, capacity sizing, capex/opex tag
2. Procurement
From stores or vendor; serial #, OEM, capacity recorded
3. Pre-comm tests
BDV oil, IR, ratio, polarity. Joint inspection by AE.
4. Erection
Plinth, structure, earthing (2 distinct), HG fuse, LT distribution box
5. Charging
PTW closed, charge, load gradually, verify direction
6. Operation
Monitor loading, oil level, leakage; quarterly inspection
7. Maintenance
Annual: oil filtration/replacement; biennial: detailed test
8. Failure
Burnt — root cause: external (lightning, theft) vs internal (loading)
9. Repair / Replace
Repair at vendor; replace from stores; log all part movements
10. Survey-off
End-of-life; scrap value; SBR (Survey & Beyond Repair) committee
DTR failure red flags
  • • Annual failure rate > 5% — systemic issue
  • • Same OEM repeatedly failing — quality/warranty review
  • • Specific area cluster — theft / overload / harmonics
  • • Multiple "burnt during commissioning" — installation error
DTR audit objections (top hits)
  • • Damaged DTR not surveyed off > 6 months
  • • Mismatch between SAP register & physical count
  • • Repair cost > threshold without committee approval
  • • Replacement not tracked back to scrap accounting

Index discipline

SAIDI / SAIFI / CAIDI / MAIFI — explained simply

SAIDI
System Avg Interruption Duration Index
= Σ (consumer-hours interrupted) / total consumers. "How long the average consumer was without supply."
SAIFI
System Avg Interruption Frequency Index
= Σ (interruptions experienced) / total consumers. "How many times the average consumer lost power."
CAIDI
Customer Avg Interruption Duration
= SAIDI / SAIFI. "Average time to restore once you've been hit." Measures restoration speed.
MAIFI
Momentary Avg Interruption Frequency
= Σ (momentary <1 min interruptions) / consumers. Often hidden but irritating to consumers.
Improving SAIDI

Reduce duration per outage. Tools: faster fault localization (FPI - Fault Passage Indicators), sectionalizers, ring-main reconfiguration, pre-positioned material/crew.

Improving SAIFI

Reduce number of outages. Tools: tree management, insulator coating, animal guards, conductor replacement, lightning arrestors, surge protection.

Shutdown discipline

Planned shutdowns — minimize pain, maximize work

A division that frequently does unannounced shutdowns is a division that has lost public trust. Plan early, announce widely, execute precisely.

1
Need identified — maintenance, augmentation, joint with TRANSCO, new connection cut-in
2
Date selected — preferably Sunday/holiday morning; avoid exam dates, festivals, harvest peak
3
Sanction — AE proposes, EE approves, SE informed; PTW prepared
4
Public notice — 3-4 days prior via local paper, SMS push, app notification, ward councilors
5
Critical consumer alert — hospitals, water plants, dairy, IT parks — phone confirmation
6
Material & crew pre-stage — vehicles, ladders, conductors, hardware all loaded prior evening
7
Execute — supervisor on site, EE on call, hourly status to control room
8

HR & Establishment Command

Your division's people machinery

Technical work is done by people. An EE who masters HR rules is an EE whose office runs quietly. Get one DPC wrong and you spend the next 2 years in court.

DPC = Departmental Promotion Committee

DPC workflow — get it right, or get sued

DPC errors are the #1 source of service litigation in DISCOMs. The single biggest mistake: rushing without verifying seniority + reservation + ACR + vigilance clearance for EVERY candidate.

1
Vacancy identification — cadre strength vs working strength; sanctioned post; reservation rotation
2
Eligibility list — minimum qualifying service in feeder cadre; service date as crystallized
3
Seniority list verification — circulated, objections invited, finalized; integrated list updated
4
Zone of consideration — typically 5× vacancies for select list, 1:1 for SCBA
5
Roster verification — SC/ST/BC/EWS rotation as per state rule
6
ACR scrutiny — last 5 years; benchmark "Very Good" / "Good"; integrity certified
7
Vigilance clearance — current; any pending case → sealed cover
8
DPC meeting — recommendation panel; minutes signed by all members
9
Notification — approval, posting orders, joining; date of officiation
DPC pitfalls that overturn promotions in court
  • • Wrong feeder cadre eligibility years
  • • Roster point miscounted (carry-forward, backlog confusion)
  • • ACR not communicated to officer before being adverse
  • • Sealed cover process violated
  • • Junior promoted superseding senior without recorded reasons
Strong EE practice
  • • Maintain a "promotion file" per officer with all documents
  • • Sign on each page of seniority verification
  • • Keep certified copy of roster register
  • • Never agree to verbal "adjustments"
  • • Consult law cell for borderline cases
Seniority is destiny

Seniority maintenance & reservation roster

A single mis-numbered seniority entry can cascade into hundreds of disputes 10 years later. The EE's role isn't to draft the seniority list — it's to verify and authenticate it before any DPC.

Inter-se seniority — common tie-breakers
  1. Date of regular appointment in cadre
  2. Date of recruitment (if same DOR)
  3. Marks / merit position
  4. Age (older = senior)
Reservation roster — the rotation cycle

100-point roster (state-specific). Points are reserved by category in a defined sequence. Carry-forward applies if reserved category candidate unavailable. Backlog vacancies handled separately. Mistakes cascade — verify with HR cell.

Quasi-judicial domain

Disciplinary proceedings — natural justice or nothing

Every disciplinary action must satisfy: opportunity to defend, fair inquiry, reasoned order. Skip any one — the order is set aside and the officer often gets back-wages.

Minor penalty (no inquiry usually)
  • • Censure
  • • Withholding increment
  • • Recovery of pecuniary loss
  • Process: show-cause → reply → speaking order
Major penalty (inquiry mandatory)
  • • Reduction in rank/pay
  • • Compulsory retirement
  • • Removal / Dismissal
  • Process: charge memo → reply → inquiry officer → IO report → 2nd show-cause on quantum → order
EE's role

As disciplinary authority for subordinate cadres, you must sign charges that hold up in tribunals. Common error: vague charge memos ("dereliction of duty") — name the act, date, witnesses, regulation violated. Specificity wins.

Soft skills meets law

Grievance handling & union relations

Unions are not enemies. They are organized voices. An EE who builds professional relationships with union office-bearers prevents 80% of escalations at the meeting table, not in the courtroom.

Open communication

Annual ritual

ACR / APR — write fairly, communicate adversely

The ACR (Annual Confidential Report) or APR (Annual Performance Report) is a serious document. It controls promotions, postings, vigilance clearances. Three principles:

  1. Specificity over adjectives — "Restored F-7 in 2.5 h vs 4 h division average" beats "good performer"
  2. Adverse remarks MUST be communicated — officer signs acknowledgment; gets opportunity to represent
  3. Consistency with action — you cannot say "outstanding" in ACR and refuse to recommend for promotion

Common error: misclassification

A simple test: does the work create or extend an asset, or does it maintain an existing asset?

⚠ Misclassification = audit objection. If you replace a 100 kVA burnt DTR with a 200 kVA new one, only the like-to-like value is opex; the upgrade portion is capex. Document the split.

RTI Act 2005

RTI workflow — answer or face the penalty

Each application has a 30-day clock (48 hours if life/liberty). Default = penalty on PIO personally (₹250/day, up to ₹25,000) plus disciplinary action. Don't take it lightly.

1
Receipt & registration — date stamped, RTI register entry, PIO assigned (usually section head/AE)
2
Examination of scope — what info is sought; involves how many sections; is exempted info present (Section 8/9)
3
Drafting reply — point-wise; quote section if exempted; offer inspection for voluminous
4
Approval by APIO/EE — verify factual accuracy; sign with date
5
Dispatch within 30 days — registered post or applicant pickup; copy on file
6
First appeal if any — to designated First Appellate Authority (typically SE); 30 days to dispose
7
Second appeal — to State Information Commission; PIO may be called
EE rule of thumb

If an RTI is repetitive, framed broadly, or politically motivated — answer factually anyway. Do NOT refuse without exact section. The cost of refusal (penalty + reputation) far exceeds the cost of disclosure.

Safety & Technical Sanctions Center

Zero fatalities is the only acceptable target

Every accident has a paper trail leading back to someone's neglected sign-off. This module exists so yours is not that signature.

Permit-to-Work — life-saving discipline

PTW system — every step that prevents a death

No work on energized or recently-energized equipment without a written, signed, time-bounded PTW. This is not paperwork — this is the legal and moral contract that keeps your linemen alive.

1
PTW request by Permit Receiver (PR — usually JE/Lineman in charge of work) — equipment, location, scope, crew, time
2
PTW issuer (Permit Issuer) — AE or designated officer — verifies isolation feasibility, alternate supply if needed
3
Isolation steps — break switches opened, locked off, danger boards hung, fuses removed
4
Discharge & test dead — earthing rod / discharge rod applied; multi-meter test
5
Earthing — work-side earthing applied; "Men at Work" board displayed
6
PTW signed by PR — work commences; PR personally responsible for crew
7
Work completion — PR confirms all men/material cleared; signs off PTW
8
PTW returned to issuer — earths removed, "ready to charge" confirmed
9
Charging — only after PTW cancelled and confirmed by issuer; gradual; verify load direction
PTW violations that kill people
  • • Charging without confirming all PTWs returned
  • • Verbal "go ahead" without written permit
  • • Multiple PTWs on same equipment uncoordinated
  • • Charging after auto-reclose without manual confirmation
  • • Earthing missed at work site
EE's audit habit
  • • Surprise check PTW register at SS monthly
  • • 1 random work-site visit per week
  • • Verify discharging rod present in every vehicle
  • • Quarterly mock drill on isolation procedure
  • • Safety induction for every new recruit
First 24 hours decide everything

Electrical accident — protocol

When fatal news arrives, your phone will not stop ringing. This is the moment to be calm, methodical, and humane.

Immediate (0-2 hours)
  • 1. Make supply safe — isolate, earth, cordon
  • 2. Medical aid / hospital transport (108)
  • 3. Inform police — FIR will follow
  • 4. Inform DDO (Distribution District Officer / state Electrical Inspectorate)
  • 5. Phone SE/CGM with facts only (no speculation)
  • 6. Preserve the site — no repairs, photograph extensively
Short-term (2-24 hours)
  • 1. Visit site personally + visit hospital / bereaved family
  • 2. Preliminary report to SE within 6h
  • 3. Witness statements recorded
  • 4. Material specimens preserved (burnt clamp, broken conductor)
  • 5. Sketch of accident site
  • 6. Ex-gratia process initiated (if departmental)
  • 7. DDO inquiry scheduling
Medium-term (24h - 30 days)
  • 1. Cooperate with DDO inquiry — produce records
  • 2. Internal RCA — what failed in system / SOP / training
  • 3. Disciplinary process if negligence found
  • 4. Compensation as per regulation
  • 5. Police / coroner cooperation
  • 6. Family liaison through dedicated officer
Long-term (post inquiry)
  • 1. Implement DDO recommendations
  • 2. Section-level mock drill
  • 3. Safety bulletin issued to all field staff
  • 4. Recurrence-prevention check on similar equipment
  • 5. Report in next safety review meeting
Human note

A fatality is not a paper. It is a family that lost someone. Visit the home personally. Don't send a deputy. Don't hide behind procedure. The institution heals through how its leaders show up.

Technical sanction workflow — your engineering signature

A TS (Technical Sanction) is your engineering certification that the proposal is feasible, safe, and economically justified at the estimated cost. Once you sign, you own the design responsibility.

1

2
Survey & design — line route, pole positions, DTR sizing, conductor selection, LT layout
3
Estimate preparation — quantities × SoR rates; non-SoR items priced separately
4
Technical sanction by EE — verify scope, design, costing; sign + date
5
Administrative sanction — by competent authority based on financial limit
6
Execution — work-order issued, material indented, work executed

9

Where money disappears

Estimate scrutiny — 12-point checklist

  1. Scope statement clear (what, where, why)
  2. Demand basis attached (load study, complaint history)
  3. DTR sizing justified vs sanctioned load
  4. Conductor type appropriate for span/load/area
  5. Pole spacing within span limits
  6. Earthing provisions (2 distinct earths per IS 3043)
  7. Right-of-Way feasibility (no land dispute)
  8. Statutory clearances mapped (forest, railway, road)
  9. Quantities multiplied by current SoR rates
  10. Non-SoR items have separate analysis + market quotes
  11. Contingency / supervision charges as per norm (typically 5-10%)
  12. Departmental vs contract execution decision recorded
SoR = the price book

Schedule of Rates — read it, interpret it correctly

The SoR (sometimes called SSR — Standard Schedule of Rates) is a state-level rate book published annually for items like conductor stringing, DTR erection, pole erection, LT distribution, earthing, hardware. The EE must:

  • • Use the current year SoR — outdated SoR is an audit objection magnet
  • • Understand bundled vs unbundled items — e.g., "DTR erection 100 kVA all-inclusive" vs "labor only"
  • • Apply correct unit — running meter vs km; per piece vs per kg
  • • Non-SoR items — separate analysis from market rates, approved by competent authority
  • • Escalation / price variation — applicable as per contract; track major commodity prices
Contractor safety = your liability

Contractor & departmental crew safety

In law, if a contractor's worker dies during work for the DISCOM, the principal employer (DISCOM/EE) has liability — even when the contractor is also liable. Hence:

  • • Pre-qualification of contractors — safety record, PPE compliance, insurance
  • • Mandatory PPE — helmet, gloves, safety belt, shoes, earthing rod issued and signed
  • • Training & induction — before work commences
  • • PTW discipline — same as departmental; no shortcut
  • • Surprise checks — site safety audit by AE/EE
  • • Insurance compliance — workman compensation policy active throughout
  • • Incident reporting — contractor accidents also count in safety stats; full inquiry

    Charge & Role

    1–3 · The chair you sit in

    DE is the technical and executive unit of the system, reporting to the Superintending Engineer. Owns all field works, supports commercial/industrial/rural development, cooperates with the Senior Accounts Officer, and enforces financial discipline downstream.

    Clause 1 — DE's mandate▸

    Execute and manage all technical & field works in jurisdiction. Assist commercial work. Keep self and SE informed of commercial, industrial and rural development. Ensure prompt staff compliance with Circle Office instructions on financial & accounting matters.

    Clause 2 — Incumbency register▸

    Circle Office maintains register of incumbents for all DISCOM officers / Accounts staff. DE maintains a similar register for subordinate executive staff in his office.

    Clause 3 — Charge handover (a–e)▸
    (a) Taking over: Examine works, count/weigh/measure selected stores to verify accounts, inspect works-in-progress for quality & sanctioned-plan compliance, go through books/registers/ledgers, report arrears or confusion. Three-month rule — if relieving officer fails to flag a defect within ~3 months, he becomes responsible for it.
    (b) Handing over: Relieved officer must give a memorandum: works in hand, pending orders, important matters on operation/maintenance/load, peculiarities needing attention.
    (c) Timing: No delay in making over after successor arrives. Cannot leave station without immediate-superior permission. Relieving officer's monthly accounts cover the full month of relief; relieved officer remains answerable for transactions during his incumbency.
    (d) Prolonged transfer: If two officers draw pay simultaneously (FR 107) — SE judges if time taken is reasonable. If excessive, relieving officer treated as on leave / joining time for the excess. On death or sudden departure, next senior officer assumes charge.
    (e) Continuity: SE and AO must make special mention of cases under settlement in their transfer notes. Questionnaire forms (approved by SE) are guides — not exhaustive.

    4–8 · People, land, plans, accidents

    4 · Appointing authority for O&M▸

    DE generally controls, appoints and dismisses O&M establishment staff for whom he is the appointing authority.

    5 · Buildings & lands▸

    Responsible for preserving all buildings and works. Maintain accurate plans of all DISCOM lands on registers; ensure subordinates know the boundaries.

    6 · Land demarcation▸

    All lands to be demarcated where not done — by DISCOM subordinates in consultation with Revenue Department officers.

    7 · Records on file▸
    • (a) Accurate plans of all electrical & mechanical equipment
    • (b) Maps of distribution + tapping arrangements + feeder extensions
    • (c) Copies of all specifications issued
    • (d) All estimates sanctioned year-on-year
    8 · Reporting loss of immovable property▸

    Any serious loss of immovable property by accident or unusual occurrence must be reported immediately to the SE, per AP Financial Code rules.

    Accidents · Sanctions · Reporting

    9–12 · Don't cross these lines

    9 · Fatal accident timeline▸
    CRITICAL
    Investigate departmental fatal accidents within 5 days.
    Send investigation report within 15 days from accident date to CE/Operation and SE.
    10 · Engineering & rates▸

    DE is responsible for the engineering features of designs and the rates in estimates prepared or forwarded by him.

    11 · Sanction discipline▸

    Prohibited from starting any work or spending public funds without competent-authority sanction. Cannot deviate from sanctioned designs except trifling cases — or in emergency.

    12 · Interruptions reporting▸

    All interruptions to works in progress or to electric power service must be reported immediately to the SE, with probable cause and duration explained.

    Technical · Commercial · Financial

    13–16 · The review discipline

    13 · Charge & financial soundness▸

    DEs are in entire charge of all works in their jurisdiction. Duty: ensure administration is financially sound within their areas.

    14(a) · Technical & commercial▸

    Review register of works. Check-measure as many major works as possible concurrently. Verify designs/specs followed; deviations approved by competent authority. Inspect all HT services at least once every six months and report important features to SE. Ensure ADEs realise commercial activity as a key load-development lever.

    14(b) · Financial▸

    ADEs are primarily responsible for finance, expenditure, revenue. During inspections DE checks initial records: works, stores, spares, tools & plant, imprest accounts, measurement books, collections, petty cash. Communicates negligence/oversight that may cause revenue or property loss promptly to SE. Reviews register of meters, history of services. Primarily responsible for spending of appropriations — no excesses or lapses.

    15 · Check-measure quota▸

    Check-measure important works (e.g., conductor utilisation) — at least 24 works per year. Maintain register for the purpose and produce it to Audit.

    16 · Works accounts hygiene▸

    Ensure ADEs / Section Officers maintain initial works accounts properly. Don't let field officers keep works accounts open long — that lets discrepancies creep in unnoticed.

    Meter Reading & Energy Audit

    17–19 · Safeguarding revenue

    17 · MRB review cadence▸
    (a) LT services (non-high-value), Cat I, II, VII — half-yearly
    (b) LT HV services & all industrial HT readings — quarterly + 40 check-readings/month
    (c) 1000 KVA & above — monthly + check-readings for all services in one year
    18 · Exception lists (>3 repeats/yr)▸
    • (a) Consumption too high (>120% HV)
    • (b) Consumption too low (<80% HV)
    • (c) Readings not furnished
    • (d) Door lock
    • (e) Disconnected services with progressive readings (LV)
    • (f) Negative readings (LV)
    • (g) Consumption comparison per HP for similar units
    • (h) Meter stuck-up (LV)
    • (i) Burnt (LV)
    • (j) Services not exist (LV)
    19 · Energy audit▸

    Conduct energy audit sub-division wise, section wise, 11 KV feeder wise, distribution transformer wise. Arrive at line losses. Take remedial action to plug sources and reduce them. Furnish division losses to SE/Operation. Guidelines: Memo No. CMD/EPDCL/VSP/GM(EA)/F.42/D.No.2665/03 dt. 13-09-03 (Annexure 14).

    ERO & Field Inspection

    20–21 · Boots on ground

    20 · ERO administrative head▸

    DE is administrative head of EROs. Hold monthly coordination meetings with ERO staff & field officers — purposeful, effective, aimed at performance and revenue improvement. Inspect one ERO / sub-ERO per month to verify internal checks for billing, assessment, realisation.

    21 · Inspection cadence▸
    • All sub-division offices — once/yr
    • 33/11 KV substations — once / 6 months
    • HT services up to 1 MVA as CMD — once / 6 months
    • Intensive inspections with all ADEs & SOs — ≥4 days/month
    • Theft investigation: cases between ₹5,000 – ₹10,000
    Reporting & Service Delivery

    22–26 · Numbers, calls, transformers, feedback

    22 · P&L statements▸

    Prepare P&L statement for each section, sub-division, and division — every month.

    23 · MRB monthly review▸

    Arrange MRB review every month by ADEs/AAEs to confirm consumption billed for stuck-up / meter-changed services is correct. Send report in prescribed format to AAO (ERO).

    24 · Call centres / CSC▸

    Inspect all call centres / customer service centres in jurisdiction once a fortnight. Ensure grievances are redressed within prescribed time, including new service from existing line within 48 hours.

    25 · DTR replacement SLA▸
    Maintain sufficient healthy DTRs in headquarters for failure replacement.
    Transport & erection — at DISCOM cost only. Consumer not to pay.
    Inspect LT lines & DTR structures, rectify defects before replacing sick units.
    Urban: replace within 12 hours. Rural: 24 hours.
    26 · Consumer feedback on DTR replacement▸

    Interact with consumers and obtain feedback on failed-DTR replacement — whether transformers were replaced within resolved time, and whether any farmer/consumer incurred expenditure in the replacement.

    Circle Office · DE (Technical)

    Duties & responsibilities to the SE

    The DE (Technical) is the SE's right hand at Circle Office — covering Technical, Commercial, Purchase and Administration wings, plus stores and field coordination.

    (a) Scope of office wings▸

    Looks after Technical, Commercial, Purchase & Administration wings of the SE/Operation office.

    (b) Field coordination▸

    Effectively coordinate with field DEs on important matters: replacement of defective meters, monitoring/replacement/purchase of spares, repairs of all failed transformers — in coordination with DE/MRT.

    (c) District Stores control▸

    District Stores under administrative & technical control of DE (Tech) where stores are at Circle HQ. If stores are not at Circle HQ, territorial DE/Operation carries out the same functions — check-measure, judicious issue control, send returns. Check-measurement of Power & Distribution Transformers, Meters, Switchgear, metering cubicles, CTs, PTs received at District Stores is done by DE/MRT of the respective Circle.

    (d) Field DE powers▸

    DE (Tech), Operation Circle also exercises the powers exercised by other field DEs in respect of their functions.

    (e) Personnel Officer▸

    Personnel Officer in Central Office works under DE (Tech)'s administrative control.

    (f) R&C surprise checks▸

    Conduct surprise checks. Ensure Regulation & Control (R&C) orders notified by the DISCOM are strictly implemented by field officers.

    HT Metering

    1 · HT Metering

    (a) Testing calendar▸

    Draw a calendar of testing for all HT services and ensure periodical testing is implemented. Schedule seasonal loads — ice factories, khandasari, sugar — for testing in the first month of the season.

    (b) Personal attendance — 1000 KVA+▸

    Attend personally to periodical testing of meters with CMD 1000 KVA and above.

    (c) HT inspection cadence▸
    • CMD ≥ 1 MVA — once / 6 months
    • CMD 500 KVA – 1 MVA — once / year
    • All DISCOM upkeep guidelines implemented
    (d) Metering failure restoration▸

    Inspect all HT services where metering system is reported failed. Rectify defective metering and restore within 24 hours preferably — not later than 3 days.

    (e) New HT release▸

    Attend personally the testing & release of all new HT services and ensure all DISCOM upkeep guidelines for HT services are implemented.

    LT Metering

    2 · LT Metering

    (a) CT-services calendar▸

    Draw calendar and ensure periodical testing of LT services with CTs — once per year.

    (b) Stores meter testing▸

    All meters received in stores are tested as per standards.

    (c) Sick-meter rectification▸

    Procure required spares; arrange rectification of all sick meters.

    (d) Rolling stock▸

    Maintain rolling stock of HT meters, LT meters, CTs, PTs and CT-PT sets.

    Sub-Station

    3 · Sub-Station

    (a) Relay testing▸

    All relays in substations tested once every year.

    (b) Relay settings approval▸

    Approve relay settings at all 33/11 KV substations — consider fault levels and settings approved by SE/Grid Operation for upstream equipment.

    (c) Substation meter testing▸

    Test all meters at substations once / 6 months — especially energy meters for proper energy audit.

    (d) Protective equipment commissioning▸

    Test and commission protective equipment of all breakers, power transformers, capacitor banks etc.

    (e) VCB high-voltage test▸

    Test vacuum circuit breakers with HV test kit — once a year.

    (f) Substation inspection▸

    Inspect all sub-stations once / 6 months — check earth resistance, condition of station batteries & chargers etc.

    Power Transformer

    1 · Power Transformer

    (a) Joint failure inspection▸

    Conduct joint inspection of all failed power transformers, breakers etc. — determine damage extent and repair cost.

    (b) Repair SLA▸

    Ensure all failed PTs & equipment are repaired within 3 months from failure date — pursue with companies and headquarters.

    (c) Movement & replacement▸

    Arrange movement of power transformers and replacement at substations.

    (d) Personal attendance — 5 MVA+▸

    Personally attend handling of 5 MVA and above PTs during loading, transport and erection.

    (e) Private repairer audit▸

    Inspect private PT repairers frequently — not less than once in 2 months. Ensure repair quality.

    (f) Thermometers & Buchholz▸

    All PT thermometers working correctly — calibrate and check Buchholz relay operation once a year.

    (g) Oil filtration▸

    Arrange filtration of oil whenever testing warrants it — or at least once in 5 years.

    (h) OLTC oil condition▸

    Check On-Load Tap-Changer oil condition and replace if required.

    (i) Oil leakages & complaints▸

    Attend oil leakages and other complaints of power transformers promptly.

    Distribution Transformer

    2 · Distribution Transformer

    (a) Rolling stock & survey▸

    Maintain rolling stock of DTRs in sub-division. Arrange survey-reporting for non-repairable units.

    (b) DTR repair SLA▸

    Failed DTRs repaired within 30 days.

    (c) Contractor / SPM quality▸

    Check repairs at contractors and SPM centres for quality. Verify actual work done vs. assessment / joint inspection by ADEs. Ensure correct coil weights replaced & work documented.

    (d) SPM centre inspection▸

    Inspect all SPM centres and contract repair units. Ensure testing equipment is in good working condition and all required tests are performed per standards.

    Sub Stations

    3 · Sub Stations

    (a) Failure investigation▸

    Investigate failure of all equipment — PTs, breakers, capacitor banks etc. Inspect installation within 48 hours of failure. Send detailed investigation report to headquarters.

    (b) Substation inspection cadence▸
    • Capacity ≥ 8 MVA — once / quarter
    • Lower capacity — once / 6 months
    • Send detailed maintenance report
    (c) PT maintenance schedule▸

    Ensure quarterly / half-yearly / annual maintenance schedules for Power Transformers are carried out as per schedule (Annexure).
